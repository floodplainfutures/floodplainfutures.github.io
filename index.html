Yolo Basin Bypass · Yolo County, California

Seasonal
by Design

A flood control structure, a migratory bird corridor, and a working agricultural landscape. The Yolo Basin Bypass does all three at once, every year yet largely unseen by the communities it sustains.

Migration / Over 200 species of waterfowl and shorebirds use the basin as a critical stopover on the Pacific Flyway, one of four major North American bird migration corridors, stretching from Alaska to Patagonia.
Season / The basin has no fixed state. In winter it floods. In spring it drains. In summer it grows rice and tomatoes. In fall it fills again. A cycle that repeats every year.

Historical context

1911 Bypass authorized by the California Debris Commission following catastrophic flooding
1932 Construction completed. Bypass designed to carry 300,000+ cfs of Sacramento River overflow
1990s Yolo Basin Foundation established. Managed wetland program begins under ecological mandate
2005 Yolo Bypass Wildlife Area expands. Rice field managed flooding formally integrated with waterfowl habitat planning
2017 Oroville Dam crisis. Bypass activated continuously for 73 days, its largest modern activation
2023 Atmospheric river sequence. Largest recorded flood extent in over a decade

Winter Sandhill cranes at dusk, northern pintails, tundra swans, dunlin flocks visible from County Road 32A. Peak waterfowl counts Nov–Jan.
Spring Shorebirds moving through during field draw-down. Tricolored blackbird colonies. Western meadowlarks along levee roads through April.
Summer White-faced ibis in rice fields, black-necked stilts along irrigation channels, American bittern in tule margins. Early morning recommended.
Fall First returning ducks, fields beginning to flood. Greatest species diversity of the year. Overlap of late migrants, early winter arrivals, and resident species.
