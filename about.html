design as environmental interpretation

A thesis exploring how information design can reshape the way people perceive and relate to ecological systems—transforming institutional environmental data into legible, emotionally resonant, and ethically grounded forms.

the project

This thesis sits at the intersection of design, environmental data, and public understanding. It investigates how visual systems—maps, timelines, zines, interactive web interfaces, and speculative artifacts—can translate complex environmental data into forms accessible to non-expert audiences.

Design as research, not decoration. The work uses the Yolo Basin's water systems and flood data as a case study—reinterpreting data collected by scientific institutions to foreground context, history, human impact, and lived experience rather than abstraction alone.

drawing from

  • Information design and data visualization
  • Environmental humanities and ecological thinking
  • Citizen science and participatory knowledge
  • Critical design and feminist/Indigenous approaches to knowledge (situated knowledge, relationality, care)

Rather than prioritizing efficiency or neutrality, the work questions who environmental data is designed for, what is lost when data is stripped of place and narrative, and how alternative visual forms can act as counter-archives to dominant scientific representations.

thesis outputs

The final work includes multiple components designed to explore environmental data through different lenses and formats:

  • A written component (literature review + critical reflection)
  • Visual literature-review zines that synthesize research through design
  • A historical timeline linking design history and environmental events
  • A web-based interactive visualization using real datasets from eBird and USGS

Together, these outputs argue that design can function as an interpretive and ethical layer between environmental data and the public—shaping not just understanding, but responsibility, memory, and care.

the yolo basin case study

The Yolo Basin serves as a living case study—a seasonal floodplain in Northern California where water, land, and ecological data intersect. By visualizing flood patterns alongside bird migrations, the project demonstrates how design can reveal relationships invisible in raw datasets.

Data from eBird and USGS is recontextualized to tell a story about place, seasonal rhythms, and the interdependence of water systems and migratory life—moving beyond institutional abstraction toward situated, experiential knowledge.

data sources & tools

  • eBird citizen science observations (2004–2026)
  • USGS National Water Information System (Station 11453000)
