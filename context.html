MFA Design Thesis · UC Davis · 2026 Zahra Baxi

floodplain futures

An interactive design exploration of the Yolo Basin as a dynamic landscape shaped by flood infrastructure, migratory bird ecologies, and agriculture. This project visualizes how seasonal change transforms the basin, and how these overlapping systems can be better understood through interaction.

01

premise

The Yolo Basin is not a static landscape, it is a system that expands, floods, feeds, and recedes with the seasons.

Located just outside of Davis, California, the basin operates as both critical flood infrastructure and a productive agricultural landscape. During periods of heavy rain, water from the Sacramento River is diverted into the basin, transforming fields into temporary wetlands.

This seasonal flooding supports ecological processes along the Pacific Flyway, where migratory birds rely on the basin as a resting and feeding ground. At other times of year, the same land is cultivated for crops such as rice, creating a continuous cycle between water, habitat, and food production.

Despite its complexity, these systems are often experienced separately rather than as an interconnected whole.

This project begins from the premise that understanding the basin requires seeing these systems together, as a single, dynamic environment shaped by seasonal change.

02

the place

41 mi length of bypass
59,000 acres total
200+ bird species / year
3.2 mi from Davis, CA

The Yolo Basin is a designed floodplain where infrastructure, ecology, and agriculture coexist.

Stretching along the Sacramento Valley, the Yolo Bypass carries excess water away from urban areas during flood events. Structures like the Fremont Weir allow water to spill into the basin, reducing pressure on the Sacramento River.

For much of the year, the basin appears dry and agricultural. During the wet season, these same fields transform into wetlands, supporting migratory birds traveling along the Pacific Flyway.

infrastructure

The basin diverts excess water from the Sacramento River to prevent flooding in nearby cities.

ecology

Seasonal flooding creates temporary wetlands that support diverse ecosystems.

agriculture

During dry months, the basin operates as productive farmland, especially for rice cultivation.

migration

The basin provides critical habitat for migratory birds along the Pacific Flyway.

03

the question

If the basin is defined by change, how can it be understood and experienced?

Flood, migration, and agriculture operate across time and scale, making them difficult to perceive in everyday experience.

?

How can interaction design make visible the seasonal systems of the Yolo Basin?

This project frames the basin as a design challenge: how to communicate complex environmental systems through interaction.

05

thesis

The Yolo Basin is a dynamic system shaped by seasonal change.

thesis statement

This thesis proposes that interaction design can reveal the interconnected systems of flood, migration, and agriculture within the Yolo Basin, reframing it as a living, adaptive landscape shaped by both infrastructure and ecology.

By visualizing these systems together, the project highlights their interdependence and invites new ways of understanding environmental change.
